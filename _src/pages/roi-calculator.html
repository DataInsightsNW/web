Reading the number properly

What this figure is, and what it isn't.

A calculator that only ever flatters the pitch isn't worth much. Here's how to treat the result.

It's a cost, not a cheque

Automating the report frees the time; it doesn't post the money back into your bank account. The gain is real, but it shows up as capacity — that person doing something more useful — rather than as a refund.

Automation is rarely 100%

Most reporting keeps a few minutes of human judgement: a sense-check, a comment on the exceptions. Assume you recover most of the time, not all of it.

The error cost isn't in here

This only counts time. It doesn't price a decision made on a wrong number, a month-end restated, or a customer invoiced incorrectly — often the bigger cost, and the harder one to estimate.

There's a build cost too

Setting the automation up is a one-off fixed price. Compare that against the annual figure above rather than treating the saving as free.

Some reports shouldn't be automated

If a report runs twice a year and takes an hour, leave it alone. Automation earns its keep on frequency and repetition, and we'll tell you when it doesn't.

Your hourly cost may be higher

If the person rebuilding the report is a manager or a finance lead rather than an administrator, push the rate slider up. That's often where the real number hides.
